# Deep Learning Platform for Optimizing Forming Tool Design

- **Citation (IEEE):** B. K. Shah, M. Ganapathy, P. Biswas, "Deep Learning Platform for Optimizing Forming Tool Design," *Trans. Indian Inst. Met.*, vol. 79, art. 130, 2026, doi: 10.1007/s12666-026-03925-7.
- **Domain:** Sheet metal forming (stamping die design), surrogate modelling / design optimisation
- **Relevance to FYP (1-5):** 3 — sheet-metal manufacturing with a deep-learning surrogate + gradient-based optimisation loop; transferable to process optimisation, but no LLM, no fault diagnosis, no laser cutting.

## Problem
Stamping die design relies on iterative CAD + FEA runs that are slow and expensive and may not explore the design space. Prior deep-learning platforms (Attar et al.) sampled geometry uniformly, so fine features in high-curvature regions (where cracks/thinning defects arise) were poorly represented.

## Data & modalities
Synthetic data only: 100 parametrised cross-die geometries generated by Latin Hypercube Sampling (three radii varied 10–26 mm) and their FEA forming simulations (thinning/strain fields). Inputs are 3D geometry (SDF samples, 32,000 points per shape) and 2D height maps; outputs are 2D thinning maps. Split 80/10/10.

## Method (architecture: encoders, fusion, LLM/VLM used, RAG/KG, fine-tuning e.g. LoRA, prompting)
- **Generator:** DeepSDF MLP (8 layers, 512 hidden, softplus, skip connection) with a 256-D latent code per die; trained explicitly (SDF regression) and implicitly (Eikonal + normal constraints). Curvature-weighted surface sampling (discrete mean curvature via trimesh) puts more samples in critical regions. Marching Cubes reconstructs the mesh; PyTorch3D renders a height map.
- **Surrogate:** Res-SE-U-Net mapping height map + blank mask to a 2D thinning map (MSE loss, 1000 epochs).
- **Optimisation:** gradient descent on the latent vector only. The loss combines a similarity term to the start latent, a ReLU constraint on the mean of the top-5% thinning (threshold 0.10), and latent L2 regularisation.
- No LLM, RAG or prompting.

## Results (key numbers and metrics)
- Surrogate R² = 0.9997 (train), 0.9984 (test).
- Implicit generator reconstructed shapes better than the explicit one.
- Cross-die optimisation over 200 iterations: maximum thinning fell from ~18% to 15.6%, and the top-5% average thinning fell from 15.5% to 13.2%.

## Limitations
Synthetic and small (100 shapes, one cross-die family). Process parameters and material were held constant. Thinning is the only constraint. Optimised shapes were not validated experimentally. Loss weights were set by trial and error.

## Transferable ideas for the FYP pipeline
- **Process optimisation / LLM reasoning:** a differentiable surrogate (process parameters → quality metric such as dross height, kerf width or bevel-angle error) can be paired with gradient or Bayesian search. The LLM then explains and justifies the recommended parameter changes rather than inventing them.
- **Tabular/time-series:** build the surrogate from TruLaser machine-database records (power, speed, gas pressure, focus position, nozzle, material/thickness) mapped to defect outcomes.
- **Robust objective design:** optimise on a percentile metric (top-5% average) rather than a peak value. This is useful for noisy defect measurements.
- **Data sampling:** the weighted-sampling idea of putting more samples in critical regions carries over. For example, oversample bevel corners and contour transitions where defects cluster.
- **Evaluation:** report R² and error histograms, and inspect the worst cases.
