# Teaching multimodal LLMs to comprehend 12-lead electrocardiographic images

- **Citation (IEEE):** R. Liu, Y. Bai, X. Yue, et al., "Teaching multimodal LLMs to comprehend 12-lead electrocardiographic images," *npj Digital Medicine*, vol. 9, Art. no. 349, 2026, doi: 10.1038/s41746-026-02551-3.
- **Domain:** Medical imaging / domain instruction-tuning of MLLMs (cardiology)
- **Relevance to FYP (1-5):** 4 - medical domain, but it is a full recipe for adapting an open MLLM to a specialist image type: synthetic images with realistic distortions, a 1M instruction-tuning set with LLM quality control, an expert benchmark and LLM-as-judge validated against experts. Each step maps onto the FYP's vision + LLM stages.

## Problem
Automated ECG models cover narrow condition sets and need raw signals, which are often unavailable when only printed or digital ECG images exist. General MLLMs interpret ECG images poorly, and there is no instruction-tuning data or standard benchmark for the task.

## Data & modalities
- **ECGInstruct:** more than 1M ECG image-text instruction pairs. Images are synthesised from raw signals, with about 1:1 clean vs. distorted images that simulate printing and scanning artefacts. Tasks cover feature recognition, rhythm, morphology and report generation. Sources span several regions. Instructions are generated by LLMs with expert and automated (LLM-scored) quality control. PTB-XL annotations were rewritten into narrative reports.
- **ECGBench:** expert-curated, with four tasks over nine datasets: abnormality detection, report generation, MMMU-ECG multiple choice and ECG Arena multi-turn. It mixes synthetic and real paper ECGs.

## Method (architecture: encoders, fusion, LLM/VLM used, RAG/KG, fine-tuning e.g. LoRA, prompting)
- **PULSE-7B** uses the LLaVA architecture: CLIP-ViT-L/14-336 vision encoder, a 2-layer MLP projector and the Vicuna-1.5-7B LLM. It is instruction-tuned (batch size 128, learning rate 2e-5, cosine schedule).
- A Qwen2-VL-7B backbone was tested for generality.
- Image encoder compared with signal encoder.
- Evaluation: AUC and F1 for classification, and GPT-4o as judge for open-ended outputs, validated against cardiologists.

## Results (key numbers and metrics)
- PULSE beats general MLLMs (GPT-4o, GPT-4o mini, Gemini, open models) by 21-33% average accuracy on out-of-domain datasets, including +21% on MMMU-ECG vs. GPT-4o.
- LLaVA backbone vs. Qwen2-VL: PTB-XL Super 76.9 vs. 75.1, PTB-XL Report 65.4 vs. 62.8, MMMU-ECG 64.0 vs. 60.4.
- The image encoder beats the signal encoder, especially out of domain.
- Only modest degradation on 150 photographed printouts.
- LLM-judge vs. cardiologist Pearson r = 0.917-0.934.
- Adding more data sources steadily improves performance.

## Limitations
- PTB-XL reports are not fully narrative and had to be rewritten by an LLM.
- Richer clinician-written reports are needed.
- The training images are largely synthetic renderings.

## Transferable ideas for the FYP pipeline
- **Data ingestion:** synthesise training images with realistic artefacts (lighting, reflections on metal, blur, phone-camera photos), mixing about 1:1 clean and distorted, to make defect-image understanding robust to shop-floor photography.
- **Vision + LLM fine-tuning:** a LLaVA-style setup (CLIP encoder + MLP projector + 7B LLM) instruction-tuned on domain image-text pairs. Build a small "LaserInstruct" set from cut-part images with tasks such as defect recognition, severity, likely-cause reasoning and report generation.
- **Data:** use an LLM to generate instruction-response pairs from expert labels and manuals, then filter them with an LLM quality score plus spot-checks by experts.
- **Evaluation:** build a small benchmark with closed tasks (classification F1/AUC, multiple choice) and open tasks (report and multi-turn diagnosis judged by an LLM). Validate the judge against expert scores with correlation, as done here.
- **Evaluation:** test robustness on real photos vs. curated images.
